# Off-Grid PV Systems for Rural Electrification

**A Resource Discussion — Case Study: Maiduguri, Northeastern Nigeria**

---

## Introduction to Place and Application

The aim of this essay is to discuss the potential of installing off-grid PV systems to deliver electricity to the rural areas of Maiduguri in the northeastern part of Nigeria. The essay explores the potential, opportunities, and challenges associated with the proposed scheme, taking into consideration the costs and benefits involved in PV system generation. Maiduguri lies approximately at 13°05'E longitude and 11°51'N latitude, with an average elevation of 354 meters above sea level.¹

The two climatic seasons are the dry season (November–March) and the wet season (April–October). Maiduguri has good availability of solar radiation, with 23 MJ/m² as the annual average of daily global irradiation measured on the horizontal surface in the state (see Appendix 1). Monthly, the average varies from 17 MJ/m² as the minimum to 25 MJ/m² as the maximum.² The mean annual rainfall is 800mm, with a mean temperature of 40°C.³ Average daily sunshine duration in Maiduguri is approximately 7.0 hours per day.

## Justification of the Choice of Renewable Resource

Nigeria is a developing country with a growing population of 174 million people. A majority of its populace, particularly in rural areas, live without access to electricity. This is because these areas are either geographically isolated or sparsely populated, and therefore located far from the national grid.⁴ This poses a serious challenge in supplying sufficient energy to the country's large population.

The current energy demand in Nigeria is estimated at over 40,000MW, with only 2,900–4,000MW being supplied to meet the country's needs. The country's high dependence on fossil fuels has led to economic and environmental impacts such as climate change and price fluctuation.⁵

According to the World Bank,⁶ solar PV is the only technology that can function anywhere, notwithstanding geographic variations in the resource (the sun, in this case). For this reason, off-grid PV systems are considered a viable option for electricity supply in remote areas. The choice of this renewable resource is mainly because solar energy is not only an alternative energy option but also has a positive environmental and economic impact.

## Analysis of the Available Resource

Nigeria has high potential to harness solar energy because it falls within the tropics of Cancer and Capricorn, where sunlight is abundant.⁷ A study evaluating solar radiation across the country found that mean solar radiation potential in Nigeria's northern regions ranged from approximately 5.62–7.01 kW/m².

The research established insolation values across four locations in Nigeria — Lagos, Enugu, Abuja, and Maiduguri — using meteorological data from the NASA research center. Insolation value indicates the amount of radiation in a given geographical location. The study found the monthly average insolation incident on a horizontal surface to be highest in Maiduguri, at approximately 6.75 kWh/m² per day, and lowest in Lagos at 4 kWh/m² per day.⁸ This suggests that installing PV systems in Maiduguri will yield an optimum energy supply due to high solar energy availability, as shown in Figures 2 and 3.

## Available Technologies to Exploit the Resource

Solar technologies currently in use are classified into three generations. First-generation systems use crystalline and multi-crystalline silicon materials; these cells currently dominate the commercial market but are expensive to produce and have relatively low efficiency. Second-generation systems are based on thin-film PV technologies (a-Si), micromorph silicon (a-Si/μc-Si), Cadmium-Telluride (CdTe), Copper-Indium-Selenide (CIS), and Copper-Indium-Gallium-Diselenide (CIGS) — these are cheaper to produce but have lower efficiency. Third-generation systems are based on organic, inorganic, and hybrid thin-film materials, offering high efficiency and low manufacturing cost; however, these technologies are not yet widely commercialized.⁹,¹⁰,¹¹

There are two types of PV systems: grid-connected systems, which are connected to the utility grid, and off-grid systems, which supply power to a facility for immediate use. Grid-connected systems are typically used for residential and commercial facilities. Both system types are based on first-generation technology.¹²

Off-grid systems are used in a variety of applications, particularly in areas where traditional infrastructure is unavailable. PV systems are reliable and require relatively little maintenance, since their output depends only on variations in solar exposure rather than mechanical wear. They generally require a battery for storage and to regulate power output. Due to the decline in PV system costs, installations are now increasingly cost-effective, which is particularly beneficial for remote areas where off-grid systems can be installed.¹³

## Socio-Economic Benefits and Costs

The potential for electricity generation from solar energy in this context is immense. As noted, Maiduguri is located in the northeastern region of Nigeria, where sunshine reaches a maximum of about 7.0 kWh/m² per day.⁸ This implies that installing off-grid PV systems as a means of harnessing solar energy will reduce the region's dependency on fossil fuel. Additionally, the deployment of off-grid PV systems can be considered an important step in improving the socio-economic conditions of rural areas, and thus overall quality of living standards. As in other rural areas in the northeastern region, agriculture is the predominant activity, and household energy demand is mainly for cooking, agricultural, and small-scale industrial activities.¹⁴ Integrating PV systems into rural settlements offers various socio-economic benefits, such as job creation and improved public health.¹⁵

The socio-economic benefits of PV systems can be summarized as: provision of job opportunities, access to information technology in rural areas, improved standard of living, reduced dependency on fossil fuel, diversification and security of electricity supply, and increased electrification of rural areas.¹⁶,¹⁷,¹⁸ However, it should be noted that these benefits could be offset by a lack of routine maintenance and security. As a result, skilled professionals are required to install and maintain solar PV systems, since comprehensive technical knowledge is necessary for their efficient application.¹⁹

## Environmental Benefits and Impacts

Off-grid PV systems provide environmental benefits compared to traditional energy sources such as fossil fuels, contributing to sustainable growth. Although they contribute significantly to reducing greenhouse gas emissions, it should be noted that these technologies also carry negative environmental consequences, which could hinder their adoption among end users.

Ecosystems are likely to be affected through the installation and transportation of PV systems. Additionally, installing PV systems on arable land could compromise the productivity of such areas, potentially causing tension and disagreement among landowners.¹⁸

PV systems do not emit pollutants during operation, although there is a potential risk of releasing toxic substances as a result of abnormal operation, which could pose a threat to human health if mitigation measures are not in place. Application of PV systems in areas with scenic landscapes can have a negative visual impact on aesthetic value, potentially affecting tourism. Nevertheless, these impacts are generally minor and can be minimized through appropriate mitigation measures. There are also several employment benefits during both the construction and operational phases.¹⁷,¹⁸

*Estimates of PV modules are based on current market prices.²²*

Assuming variable O&M costs of 10% of CapEx: $525,600 × 10% = **$52,560/year**.

Most PV system batteries have a lifespan of 5 to 7 years, so replacement costs must be factored into the system's overall economics.²⁴,²⁵ This means the battery may be replaced approximately 4 times over the 25-year life of the system:

$168,000.00 × 4 = **$672,000**

**Total cost of the PV system over 25 years:**
$525,600.00 + $52,560 + $672,000 = **$1,250,160.00**

**Electricity output calculation** (peak sun hours = 6.75 hrs/day; assumed depth of discharge = 80%²⁶):

Electricity generated = 150 kWp × 6.75 hrs × 0.8 × 365 days × 25 years = **7,391,250 kWh** (total, over 25 years)

Annual electricity yield = 7,391,250 kWh ÷ 25 years = **295,650 kWh/year**

**Cost of electricity** = Total system cost ÷ total electricity output
= $1,250,160.00 / 7,391,250 kWh = **$0.17/kWh**

**Payback period** = Total installation cost ÷ (annual electricity yield × cost of electricity)
= $525,600.00 / (295,650 × $0.17) = **≈10 years**

Assuming a feed-in tariff of $0.13/kWh²⁷ for off-grid PV systems:
Payback period = $525,600.00 / (295,650 × $0.13) = **≈14 years**

A payback period of 10–14 years indicates that the proposal to install off-grid PV systems is economically viable, since most PV systems come with a 25-year standard warranty.

It is also worth noting the potential for further cost reductions: according to the International Energy Agency (IEA),²⁸ the cost of PV cells and modules fell rapidly from $4/W in 2008 to $0.8/W in 2012, and is expected to fall further to $0.3–$0.4/W by 2035.

## Final Appraisal

This essay reviewed the installation of off-grid PV systems in a rural area of Nigeria, including their socio-economic and environmental benefits and economic viability. Results show a payback period of 10 to 14 years over a 25-year system lifespan, indicating that the proposed installation of off-grid PV systems in the rural community of Maiduguri is economically viable.

Although solar energy technologies are being developed in Nigeria, most PV modules must still be imported, driving up investment costs.²¹ To address this, government subsidies could help reduce the high cost of importing solar PV systems.

Maiduguri has high solar energy potential, and the deployment of off-grid PV systems in this area offers numerous benefits that could improve both the environment and local economic activity. Combined with the availability of solar energy in the location and the declining cost of PV systems, off-grid PV represents a strong renewable energy option for rural electrification in this region.
